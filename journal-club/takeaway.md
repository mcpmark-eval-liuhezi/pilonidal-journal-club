Journal club heads-up: this Thursday we're covering Tokocin et al., "Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020)" (International Wound Journal, 2026; PMID 42633928). It's a single-centre Turkish series of 2222 patients with primary pilonidal sinus disease operated between January 2006 and December 2020, with a minimum 5-year follow-up, comparing nine surgical techniques. Headline findings: 5-year cumulative recurrence 7.9% (175/2222) and postoperative infection 6.9% (154/2222), with overall recurrence of 10.2% (226/2222); outcomes differed significantly between techniques (both p < 0.001), and the lowest recurrence rates were with the Karydakis flap (1.5%), Bascom cleft lift (2.3%) and Dufourmentel flap (2.4%) — versus lay-open treatment, Karydakis HR 0.19 (95% CI 0.12-0.30). Most recurrences happened within the first 2 years (median 20 months), and higher BMI was independently associated with recurrence risk (HR 1.05 per kg/m2). Come ready to argue whether off-midline flaps should be our default for primary disease, and how the newer minimally invasive options (phenol, EPSiT) fit in. Notes are in the journal club repo.

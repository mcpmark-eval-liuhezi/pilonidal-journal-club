# Journal Club Notes: Tokocin et al. 2026

## Full Citation

Tokocin, Yigitbas, Yavuz, Gulcicek. **Surgical Treatment Outcomes for Primary Pilonidal Sinus Disease: A Single-Centre Retrospective Study of 2222 Patients (2006-2020).** *International wound journal*, 2026. **PMID: 42633928**

## Key Numbers (from the record)

- **Patients:** 2222 patients with primary pilonidal sinus disease (recurrent presentations excluded)
- **Surgical techniques compared:** nine
- **Study period:** January 2006 to December 2020, single tertiary referral centre in Turkey
- **Minimum follow-up:** 5 years
- **Outcomes compared:** long-term recurrence and postoperative infection, analysed as time-to-event (Kaplan-Meier estimates and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression)
- **5-year cumulative recurrence:** 7.9% (175/2222); postoperative infection 6.9% (154/2222); overall recurrence 10.2% (226/2222)
- **Differences among techniques:** statistically significant for both recurrence and infection (both p < 0.001)
- **Lowest recurrence rates:** Karydakis flap 1.5%, Bascom cleft lift 2.3%, Dufourmentel flap 2.4%
- **Timing of recurrence:** most within the first 2 years; median time to recurrence 20 months (IQR 12-36)
- **Cox vs lay-open:** Karydakis flap HR 0.19 (95% CI 0.12-0.30), Bascom cleft lift HR 0.25 (95% CI 0.14-0.43), Limberg flap HR 0.32 (95% CI 0.19-0.53), Dufourmentel flap HR 0.26 (95% CI 0.12-0.57); higher BMI associated with higher risk of recurrence (HR 1.05 per 1 kg/m2 - abstract truncated at this point in the record)

## Where this sits in the literature

A PubMed search on pilonidal sinus disease with surgical treatment and recurrence returns a compact set of contemporaneous work (see `related-work.md`). The Tokocin paper is by some distance the largest single-centre series in that set: 2222 patients across 15 years with a minimum 5-year follow-up, against 46 patients in the comparative flap study of Punyo et al. (Cureus, 2026) and 125 patients with 1-year follow-up in the phenol-vs-Karydakis cohort of Osmanov et al. (Medicine, 2026). The surrounding literature shows two competing currents: off-midline flap reconstruction, which the Tokocin data support with markedly lower recurrence versus lay-open treatment, and the rise of minimally invasive/chemical ablation and endoscopic approaches (phenol therapy, EPSiT), which trade somewhat higher recurrence for faster healing and earlier return to work - notably in the paediatric and adolescent population covered by Colak et al. and Ehsan et al. The Tokocin series anchors the flap side of that debate with rare statistical power and long follow-up, but being retrospective and single-centre it should be read as strong observational evidence rather than a substitute for randomised comparisons, and the higher BMI risk it identifies flags a selection/modifiable-risk angle for discussion.
